Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Salem

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. Our team in Salem finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the Water SupplyWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Inside the Wall

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Decayed Wall Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan walls, floors and cabinets with moisture meters and a thermal camera to mark every wet area, including spots well away from the pipe where water has traveled along framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the framing and subfloor reach normal moisture readings, usually 3 to 5 days. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

4

Treat and Document

We apply antimicrobial treatment to affected surfaces and give you moisture logs and photos. That paperwork proves the area was dry ahead of repairs and can support a claim.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Any mold found, since it must be contained and removed before the drying can be completed.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.
  • The length of time the leak ran and the distance moisture traveled from the pipe.
