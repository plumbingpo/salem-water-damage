Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Salem Restoration Co employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Salem

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Salem Restoration Co:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Setting the Equipment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Monitoring

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.
