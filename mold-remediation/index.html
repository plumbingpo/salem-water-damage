Signs It Is Time for Mold Remediation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Discovering a plumbing or roof leak that has been dripping for a long time
  • Stains spreading on a ceiling or wall, or dark marks ringing the air vents
  • Spots you wipe away that keep reappearing in the same place
  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
  • Recent water damage or flooding that was not professionally dried
  • Fuzzy or spotted growth on stored boxes, clothing or the backs of furniture
  • Condensation forming regularly on windows, walls or cold surfaces

What a Professional Brings to Mold Remediation

Removing mold is work homeowners should not attempt on their own. When a colony is disturbed and nothing contains it, millions of spores go into the air, where they may settle in clean rooms and expose your family more than the mold did before. Professional mold remediation uses negative air pressure containment, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers and personal protective equipment so spores are captured and contained during removal.

Professional mold remediation also addresses the root cause of the growth, which is the moisture source. If the moisture is left alone, taking away what you can see only buys time, and regrowth often shows up within weeks. Our certified technicians are trained and equipped to track down moisture you cannot see, dry the affected areas the right way and make the corrections that keep mold from recurring.

How to Prevent Mold Remediation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly mold issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Use mold-resistant drywall and paint in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and basements
  • Make sure bathrooms, kitchens and laundry areas have adequate exhaust ventilation
  • Do not leave damp laundry, bath mats or spilled water sitting for long
  • Fix water leaks within 24 hours, because mold needs only moisture and 24 to 48 hours to start growing
  • Overflowing gutters send water into basements and crawl spaces, so clean them on a schedule
  • Clean and maintain your HVAC system and change filters regularly to prevent mold in ductwork
