Attic Water Damage: What Salem Homeowners Should Know

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. Our team in Salem inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

A Falling Ceiling

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Wood

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Correct the Source

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.
