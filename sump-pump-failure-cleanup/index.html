Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Salem

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Low Electrical Outlets

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.
